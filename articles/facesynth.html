FaceSynth

FaceSynth was an interactive digital musical instrument built in 2024 for the “Building Sound” course I took at the Computer Music Center at Columbia University. It translates real-time facial expressions into sound by pairing computer vision with digital signal processing, turning your webcam into a touchless expression controlled synthesizer.

The system utilizes MediaPipe Face Mesh to track facial landmarks, maps those physical coordinates into control data, and streams them over a local network using Open Sound Control (OSC) to a dedicated audio engine built with the Pyo DSP library.

System Architecture

The project is split into a modular client-server framework:

1. The Vision Client (OSC Sender)

Captures video input via OpenCV and analyzes 468+ facial landmarks using MediaPipe. It calculates geometric distances between specific face parts (like eyelids, eyebrows, and lips), normalizes the values, and packages them into OSC network packets, sending data from the following facial gestures:

X-Position

Y-Position

Z-Position

Mouth Vertical

Mouth Horizontal

Eyeclose (on or off)

Eyebrows

2. The Audio Engine Server (OSC Receiver)

Acts as the network synthesizer server. It listens on port 9000 for incoming data streams, unpacking the data sent from the client. I programmed a Pyo granular synth module to use said data to change various parameters.

Granular FaceSynth Demonstration

Your distance from the camera (Z-Position) adjusts the overall volume. Stretching your mouth wide (Mouth Horizontal) shifts the pitch of a harmony effect up or down, opening your mouth tall (Mouth Vertical) blends that harmony's volume into the mix, and raising your eyebrows (Eyebrows) increases its echoing feedback loop. Meanwhile, closing your eyes (Eyeclosed) acts as a glitch switch, freezing the audio into a stutter; when your eyes are open, moving your face (X and Y Positions) controls stereo panning and grain size, but when your eyes are closed, those same movements warp a muffled filter and add a gritty digital distortion.
